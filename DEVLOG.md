An XISO rips byte for byte on a stock drive; an XGD2 through Kreon comes out as a 360 disc of 3,820,880 sectors, locked again after. Five detection bugs came out of it:
    - Dreamcast MIL-CDs were taken for music CDs (music then a data session was always an Enhanced CD); the data session's header is checked first now.
    - CD-i only when the TOC's disc type said so; now also by its Green Book volume descriptor at sector 16. The cue parser takes CDI/2352 tracks too.
    - Super Video CDs weren't known (the check wanted VCD's MPEGAV; SVCDs have MPEG2).
    - Xbox/360 discs in a stock drive: the old check wanted the physical format to describe the whole disc, but as redumper reads them it describes only the video partition (two layers, opposite track path, a few tens of MB each). A second check takes that shape (under 256 MB on layer 0, under 512 MB in all). From redumper's layout, not seen on a disc yet; a tiny dual-layer movie disc could be taken for one.
    - PS5 discs in a USB Blu-ray drive came out as Blu-ray data; they're known by "PlayStation5" in their second sector now. A PS4 disc in a USB drive still isn't: nothing here records its layout.

## The slow MP4, solved (Oct 26, ~03:45)
- Not the encoder and not the console. FFmpeg was opened with AVFMT_FLAG_GENPTS. Twine's cells each start their clock over (cell 5's is 408.8 s behind the title's timeline), and where they do, genpts looks for a later packet with a higher DTS to give a PTS to the ones without. None comes until the new cell's clock catches up, so it read hundreds of MB ahead (530 MB at cell 5) and rescanned the whole buffer for each packet: quadratic work and growing memory, with speed falling all the way. Reproduced on the PC with the full decrypted Twine ISO (copied off the console) and a harness around remux_title, with timing every 10 s and a packet trace (pos, cell, DTS) to find it.
- Without genpts, about one packet in twenty has a DTS and no PTS:

## MKV audio gap at cell boundaries (Oct 27)
- The "audio runs 4 min long" from Oct 26 was worse than that. Read back over FTP with ffprobe, every packet of Twine.mkv: video and the English AC-3 are in sync wherever there's audio, but the audio is missing from 2538.65 s to 2912.05 s (6 min 13 s of nothing), and the last audio packet sits at 7928.1 s, 248 s after the last frame. The packets add up to 7307 s of audio for 7680 s of video.
- The log of that rip has "cell 19's clock is -373.371 s off" at 2538 s and "cell 53's clock is -247.745 s off" at the end: the same two numbers.
- Cause: where a cell restarts the clock, an AC-3 frame that began in the cell before comes out of the demuxer after the new cell has been lined up (the parser only finishes a frame when the next audio packet arrives). It still has the old cell's timestamp, so the new cell's offset puts it 373 s ahead; then fix_ts's "never go backwards" drops every real audio packet until the clock gets there.
- First try: take the cell before's clock when the new one puts a packet outside its cell. That fixed the English track but not the two unlabelled stereo ones: at cells 2, 9 and 17 the jump (39.8, 173.2, 235.8 s) is shorter than the cell, so the leftover frame landed inside the cell on the new clock and swallowed that much audio behind it.
- Fix (remux.c fix_ts): each stream keeps the offset it's on while its own timestamps carry on (under 1 s from its last packet); a stream whose clock jumps takes the cell's. A cell is lined up by the first jump of an audio or video stream that was playing as the cell began (not by a subtitle, nor by a track coming back from silence). A jump that lands more than 3 s outside its cell is a stray and is dropped alone.
- Checked on the PC (harness around remux_title, Alpine's FFmpeg 6, the full Twine ISO copied off the console): all three AC-3 tracks 240,018 frames (7680.6 s) with no gap, all 184,153 video frames, every stream 2:08:00. # Research

## Dumping PS4/PS5 discs without installing (Oct 26)
Asked again, researched again (GitHub sources read directly; PSDevWiki and archive.org were unreachable):
- No public code does it, for either console. Every dumper reads the running title's PFS mount after an install: EchoStretch's ps5-app-dumper, idlesauce's ps5-self-pager, Itemzflow ("make SURE both discs and ALL Languages are installed"), and Al-Azif's ps-disc-detect (Oct 2026: discs "identified and not dumped").
- Decrypting on a PC needs the per-title key (EKPFS, or the final XTS keys). flatz's pkg_pfs_tool and maxton's LibOrbisPkg/PkgTool decrypt retail PKGs given those keys; deriving them from a rif needs secret keys that are published only as XXXX (and the retail path is compiled out). flatz's writeup: the key "stays inside SM memory and never leaves it". PS5: SvenGDK's LibProsperoPKG documents the FIH format (FIH header | XTS outer PFS | CNT (app_sc) | install archive; a disc splits it into app_0 and app_sc per app.json, app.crc is CRC-32C per 64 KiB) and says the retail image key "is not derivable from public inputs". The rumored PS5 BootROM key leak isn't shown to give PFS or rif keys.
- Not blocked in principle: having the console decrypt in place. The kernel's PFS mount takes the encrypted keys (eekpfs, eekc from the rif, finalized, disc, pubkey_ver, key_ver, sblock_offset...) and the security processor hands back key handles, the same path an installed game uses. Public mounters (ShadowMountPlus, dump_installer, kstuff-lite's pfsc_mount: /dev/lvdctl ioctl C0286D00 then nmount fstype pfs) only ever pass fake/zero keys. Unknowns: the exact options, getting the EKC from a disc rif (npdrm mailbox), whether "disc" means a drive authentication, whether PFSC needs a second mount.
- gasb07/ps4mount (Oct 11, 2026) "installs" a PS4 PKG on a PS5 by reference (a symlink into /user/app, install metadata, sceAppInstUtilAppInstallAll) and plays it from USB without copying; retail PKGs aren't supported. Pointing it at a disc's app.pkg with the disc's rif is an untried experiment. It writes into the console's install database: ask before trying.
- The rif (kstuff-lite npdrm.h): 0x400 bytes, "RIF\0", content ID at 0x20, type/drm/content type at 0x50-0x56, discKey at 0x240, IV at 0x260, an encrypted secret at 0x270; retail and disc rifs decrypt only inside the security processor.
- So for now: install first, as ripper does. The in-place mount is the one real lead.

## Custom drive firmware (for the tester's BP50NB40)
From redumper (d9bd915b), OmniDrive (da01346b, v1.0.4), DiscImageCreator (9269234a) and the MakeMKV forum:
- OmniDrive marks itself in standard INQUIRY: bytes 74-82 "OmniDrive", 83-85 major/minor/patch (ask for 104 bytes). 1.0.4 or newer for DVDs (1.0.3 left DVD reads scrambled after one C0). SpiralDrive puts "SpiralDrive v" at bytes 36-55. A USB bridge that cuts INQUIRY to 36 bytes would hide both.
- OmniDrive's raw read is C0 (12 bytes): byte 1 disc type (0 CD, 1 DVD, 2 BD) | 0x04 raw addressing | 0x08 FUA | 0x10 descramble (DVD/BD); 2-5 start (signed), 6-9 count; byte 10 on CDs: subchannel (1 P-W, 2 Q) | 4 C2. Frames: CD 2352 (+294 C2, +96 sub), DVD 2064 (ID, IED, CPR_MAI, data, EDC; scrambled unless 0x10), BD 2070 in redumper's code (2072 in the README). A read only, by its source.
- Under OmniDrive a Nintendo disc answers plain READ with 05/30/02, and its physical format's layer descriptor starts with FF: that's how redumper tells it.
- LibreDrive has no public detection: MakeMKV is closed. It "allows one to read VID from disc" and "disables bus encryption" (its developer); turned on per session by MakeMKV, not kept in the drive.
- The BP50NB40 is MediaTek MT1959 (redumper lists two hardware kinds, NB50 and NB52). It runs LibreDrive with LG's MK-patched firmware, and OmniDrive by flashing the BU40N 1.00-based image (it then shows as BU40N 1.00). Stock 1.00 is in redumper's list as MediaTek with a 3 MB cache.
- MediaTek cache read: F1 06 <offset> <length>, 0xB00 bytes an entry (main 2352 scrambled, P-W, Q, C2), for CD lead-in/out. Only on listed MediaTek drives: F1 is READ EEPROM on Plextors.
- Raw DVD on stock LG firmware (DIC's ReadDVDRaw, the Nintendo route): A8 08 <lba> 16 (READ(12) with FUA), then 3C 02 00 for 2384 x 16 bytes (scrambled, with ECC). DIC's sheet: the BP50NB40 stalls on READ BUFFER past 3C 0B FF, so no guessing other modes.
- Never: WRITE BUFFER 3B (flashing), 3C 0D 01 on LG (Jamless Play, byte 7 FF saves to flash), FF (TSST flash, Kreon lock), EE (Plextor reset), E7 other than reading memory, SEND KEY format 06 (region), MODE SELECT with save pages.
- The drive test now: INQUIRY to 104 bytes with the OmniDrive/SpiralDrive marks; C0 raw reads only on OmniDrive; F1 only on BP50NB40/BU40N; the stock-LG raw DVD trick only on LG drives; the Nintendo check by the physical format.

## RomM upload API (5.4.x)
- Chunked upload, scope roms.write: `POST /api/roms/upload/start` with headers x-upload-platform (id), x-upload-filename, x-upload-total-size, x-upload-total-chunks; optional JSON body {"filename"} for names outside Latin-1. Returns {"upload_id"}. Then `PUT /api/roms/upload/{id}` per chunk with x-chunk-index (64 MiB cap per chunk, chunks are ceil(total/count) except the last), `POST .../complete` (201), `POST .../cancel`.
- Plain file names only: no folders in a platform upload. Multi-file rips (BIN/CUE) have to go up as one file, or as a zip.
- `POST /api/platforms {"fs_slug": "ps2"}` makes a platform that isn't there yet (platforms.write).
- `POST /api/tasks/scan {"platforms": [id]}` queues a quick scan (tasks.run), added Oct 2 and in 5.4.0. 409 when a scan is already running.
- RomM's music endpoints are for game soundtracks, not a music library. Music goes to the NAS.
